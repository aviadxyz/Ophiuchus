# Screens

Specs for the screens Rami designs (Nutrition, Physical activity, Mood, Overview, Med bot, Social), the shared data they rest on, and the intake sheet used to capture Elia's designs for Medical status and Emergency.

All numbers below marked **verify** are seed values from memory of NASA material. Check each against a NASA source before it ships, and put the source in the screen's source line.

## Shared data model

One store, read by every screen. The exact records, field names and functions are fixed in [CONTRACTS.md](CONTRACTS.md) sections 8 and 9. This section only explains the idea.

The store holds two kinds of people, described in [REAL_DATA.md](REAL_DATA.md):

- **The demo crew member.** Simulated history with a deliberate story, so a judge who opens the app cold sees full, meaningful screens.
- **Real team members.** Their own readings from their watches and whatever they enter during the weekend. Their data has gaps, and gaps are shown as gaps.

Every value carries its source (live, entered, imported, simulated) and every screen shows it.

### Seeded story

The demo crew member's history is built to give every screen something worth explaining and to make the screens agree with each other.

- 14 days ending today, generated from a fixed seed so every device shows the same data.
- Most days sit close to healthy targets with natural variation.
- Nights ending on days 9 and 10 are short, about five hours each.
- Days 10 and 11: energy intake around 70% of target and mood one to two points lower.
- Day 11: one planned exercise session skipped with the reason "Not feeling well".
- Days 12 to 14: recovery, back toward normal.
- Sodium runs over its limit on most days, which is true to life for space menus and gives the Nutrition screen a standing item.
- Today is partly filled in, as if it were mid-afternoon: breakfast and lunch logged, one of two exercise sessions done, no mood check-in yet.

"Reset demo data" on the About screen restores this.

## Nutrition

**Owner:** Rami. **Eyebrow:** `DOMAIN · NUTRITION`.

**Question it answers:** am I eating and drinking enough of the right things to stay healthy on this mission?

**Why it matters in space:** crews tend to eat less than they need in flight, and losing body mass goes with losing muscle and bone. A few nutrients behave differently in space: vitamin D (no sunlight), sodium (linked to bone loss), iron (the body stores more of it in flight), calcium, and omega-3 fats (associated with less bone loss).

**Layout, top to bottom:**

1. Header with summary pills: energy status, hydration status, number of nutrients off target.
2. Four stat tiles: Energy eaten vs target, Water vs target, Protein vs target, Sodium vs limit. Each with a seven-day sparkline.
3. "Space-critical nutrients" table in the same style as the Risks table. Columns: nutrient, why it matters in space, today's intake vs target, status pill. Rows: vitamin D, calcium, iron, sodium, omega-3, potassium. A row opens a detail drawer with the seven-day trend, the source, and food items that help.
4. Today's meals. A list of what was logged, grouped by meal. "Add food" opens a picker with the fixed space-menu list. "Add water" adds 250 ml in one tap.
5. Body mass trend for the mission so far, with a note when it drops more than a set amount from launch mass.
6. One suggestion card, generated by simple rules: for example "You are 600 kcal under target and it is 18:00. A rehydratable beef entree and a tortilla would close the gap."
7. Game link slot.

**Interactions:** add food, add water, remove an entry, open a nutrient detail. Every change updates the tiles immediately.

**Seed facts, all verify:**

| Fact | Seed value |
|---|---|
| Daily energy need | Computed per person from mass, age and sex with a standard equation and an activity factor. Roughly 2,700 to 3,700 kcal a day for most crew |
| In-flight intake | Often around 70 to 80% of need |
| Water | About 2 L a day or more |
| Vitamin D | Supplement of 800 IU a day on the station |
| Sodium | Keep under about 3,500 mg a day |
| Iron | No more than about 10 mg a day |
| Calcium | About 1,000 to 1,200 mg a day |
| Protein | About 1.2 to 1.8 g per kg body mass a day |

**Links to other screens:** low intake raises an item on Overview. Body mass trend is shared with Physical activity.

**Out of scope:** barcode scanning, custom foods, recipes.

## Physical activity

**Owner:** Rami. **Eyebrow:** `DOMAIN · EXERCISE COUNTERMEASURES`.

**Question it answers:** have I done today's exercise, and is my routine protecting my bones, muscles and heart?

**Why it matters in space:** without gravity loading, weight-bearing bones lose roughly 1 to 1.5% of their density a month and muscles shrink. Exercise is the main countermeasure. Station crews train about two hours or more a day, most days of the week, on three devices: a resistive exercise device, a treadmill with a harness, and a cycle ergometer.

**Layout, top to bottom:**

1. Header with summary pills: today's status (done, partly done, not started), sessions completed this week, streak.
2. Three stat tiles: Minutes today vs prescription, Resistive sessions this week vs plan, Aerobic sessions this week vs plan.
3. "Today's prescription". A checklist of sessions, each with device, type, duration, and target load or heart-rate zone. Each can be marked done, done with changes (enter actual minutes), or skipped with a reason.
4. "Countermeasure coverage". Three bars: bone loading, muscle, cardiovascular. Each is a simple weekly score from the logged sessions, with a status pill and one sentence on what raises it.
5. Week view: seven columns, planned vs completed minutes per day.
6. Heart rate. Live from the person's watch when a sensor is connected, simulated on the demo profile, and a "Connect sensor" empty state otherwise. No health conclusion is drawn from it.
7. Game link slot: Futvolley and Samba time count as aerobic minutes when played.

**Interactions:** mark a session, edit actual minutes, open a session detail with form tips, start a game.

**Seed facts, all verify:**

| Fact | Seed value |
|---|---|
| Daily exercise on station | About 2 to 2.5 hours including setup, 6 days a week |
| Devices | Advanced Resistive Exercise Device, T2 treadmill, CEVIS cycle ergometer |
| Bone loss without countermeasures | About 1 to 1.5% a month in hips and spine |
| Gravity for the games | Moon 1.62 m/s², Mars 3.71 m/s², Earth 9.81 m/s² |

**Links to other screens:** a skipped day raises an item on Overview. Completed minutes feed Performance. Heart rate comes from the watch.

**Out of scope:** exercise video library, custom workout builder.

## Mood

**Owner:** Rami. **Eyebrow:** `DOMAIN · MOOD`.

**Question it answers:** how have I been feeling, what seems to move it, and what small thing can I do now?

**Why it matters in space:** isolation and confinement is one of NASA's five hazards of human spaceflight. On long missions, low mood affects sleep, teamwork and performance, and the crew is far from the people and places that normally help. Regular private check-ins are part of how crews are supported.

**How it differs from Mental health:** Mood is the crew member's own quick daily diary. Mental health (unassigned) is the clinical view. Mood feeds it; it does not diagnose anything. **Confirm** this split with whoever takes Mental health.

**Layout, top to bottom:**

1. Header with summary pills: today's check-in (done or due), seven-day average, trend arrow.
2. Check-in card, the main thing on the screen. Five faces from very low to very good. After picking one, optional tags (tired, stressed, focused, calm, homesick, irritable, proud, lonely) and an optional note. One tap is enough to finish.
3. Fourteen-day timeline. One dot per check-in, colored by score, with sleep hours drawn underneath on the same axis so the link is visible.
4. "What seems to move your mood". Two or three plain sentences from simple rules on the logged data: for example "Your mood was lower on the two days after short nights."
5. "Try now" row of three small actions: a 60-second breathing exercise built into the screen, "Message home" (opens Social), "Talk to the Med bot" (opens Med bot with a gentle opening line).
6. Privacy line: "Your notes stay on this device. They are never sent to the Med bot or anyone else."
7. Game link slot.

**Interactions:** check in, edit today's check-in, tap a past dot to read its note, run the breathing exercise.

**Rules for tone and safety:**

- Never label the person. Say "lower than your usual", not "depressed".
- Three low days in a row shows a gentle suggestion to talk to the Med bot or the flight surgeon. It does not raise an emergency alert by itself.
- If a note contains words about self-harm, show a calm card pointing to the Emergency screen and the flight surgeon. Keep this simple and visible; do not try to be clever.

**Links to other screens:** reads sleep from the shared store. Sends "Talk to the Med bot" context to Med bot. Feeds Mental health, Overview and, only if the person has switched mood sharing on in Social, the mood their friends see (score word and tags, never the note).

**Out of scope:** clinical questionnaires, voice analysis. Sharing with friends is not built here; it belongs to the Social screen.

Elia designs Medical status (personal conditions) and Emergency; Rami implements them. Fill one copy of this sheet per screen with Elia, in his words, before running prompt 07. A phone photo of a paper sketch is a fine answer to question 3.

5. **What can the person do here?** List each button or action and what happens after.
6. **What should stand out first?**

### Starting questions for Medical status

- Is it a list of the person's known conditions, allergies and medications, or a live status of body systems, or both?
- Does it show the medical kit on board and what has been used?
- Who can edit it: the crew member, the flight surgeon, nobody?

### Starting questions for Emergency

- Who gets alerted: mission control, the flight surgeon, crewmates?
- One emergency type, or a choice (medical, injury, mental health crisis, environment)?
- What stops accidental presses: press and hold, or a confirm step with a countdown?
- After the alert is sent, what does the person see? First-aid steps? A line to Ophi? Their vitals?
- What does the communication delay on a Mars mission change? This is a strong story point: with a delay of up to about 20 minutes each way, the crew cannot wait for Earth, so the screen has to guide them on its own.
- How does the watch emergency button show up here?

## Overview

The landing page, and the first thing a judge sees. It is built by Rami's lane with [prompt 14](../prompts/14-overview.md), and its body figure is then redrawn to a high standard with [prompt 15](../prompts/15-body-figure.md). It carries the two things the challenge text asks for that no other screen has: a clickable human figure, and a routine that prompts the crew member through the day. See [CHALLENGE.md](CHALLENGE.md).

**Question it answers:** how am I today, which part of me needs attention, and what is still to do?

**Why it matters in space:** far from Earth, the crew member is the first person to notice a problem. A single page that shows the whole body at a glance, and what is due today, is how small things get caught before they become big ones.

**Layout, top to bottom:**

1. Header with three pills: mission day, routine progress ("3 of 7 done"), open alerts.
2. A row of five tiles: Sleep, Energy, Water, Exercise, Mood. Each opens its screen. Heart rate is shown in the routine card and in the heart system, as a reading with its source badge and no status level.
3. Two cards side by side on PC, stacked on phone, routine first:
   - **Today's routine.** What is due today, what is done, one button per item.
   - **Body.** An anatomical human figure covering sixteen body systems, each clickable, and the same sixteen as a list beside it.
4. Open alerts, if any.
5. Ophi's one-line read of the day, with "Ask the Med bot".

### Body systems

The figure covers sixteen body systems: the major ones, and the smaller ones that spaceflight is known to affect. The app measures only some of them. The others are still on the figure, because the challenge asks the tool to explain how space affects the body, and each of them says plainly that it is not tracked in this demo and how it would be checked on a real mission. No system ever shows a made-up value.

Two kinds of system:

- **Organ:** has a place on the figure and is chosen by clicking it there.
- **Whole-body:** is spread through the body (blood, immune, skin, hormones). It is chosen from a row of chips under the figure, and the figure lights up the matching layer.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `brain` | Brain and mind | Nervous | organ | `sleepHours`, `moodScore` | | `/sleep`, `/mood` |
| `eyes` | Eyes and vision | Nervous, senses | organ | not tracked | | `/risks` |
| `ears` | Balance and hearing | Nervous, senses | organ | not tracked | | `/risks` |
| `face` | Nose, sinuses and taste | Respiratory, upper | organ | not tracked | | `/nutrition` |
| `heart` | Heart and blood vessels | Cardiovascular | organ | `exerciseMinutes` | Heart rate | `/physical-activity` |
| `lungs` | Lungs and breathing | Respiratory | organ | not tracked | Blood oxygen | `/physical-activity` |
| `digestive` | Stomach, liver and gut | Digestive | organ | `energyKcal` | | `/nutrition` |
| `kidneys` | Kidneys and bladder | Urinary | organ | `waterMl`, `sodiumMg` | | `/nutrition` |
| `spine` | Spine and back | Skeletal | organ | not tracked | | `/physical-activity` |

**Status of a system.** One of five, each with its own words so color is never the only signal:

| Status | Label | Tone | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ok` | ON TARGET | `ok` | The worst level among its metrics is `ok` |
| `watch` | WATCH | `warn` | The worst level is `watch` |
| `act` | ACT | `danger` | The worst level is `act` |
| `none` | NO DATA | `neutral` | It has metrics, and every one of them has no data today |
| `untracked` | NOT TRACKED | `neutral`, dashed outline | It has no metrics in this demo |

"Worst" means `act` beats `watch`, `watch` beats `ok`, and a metric with no data is ignored unless all of them have none.

The status describes the indicators the app tracks for that system. It is not a statement about the organ. The drawer says so in one line: "Based on what you logged today. It is not a medical finding."

**Text for each system.** Three short parts, shown when the system is opened: what it does, what space does to it, and how it is checked on a mission. The third part answers the challenge's questions about which measurements can be done in space and why. All of it is general knowledge written from memory, so every line is **verify** and the screen shows "Source: to be confirmed" under it until a source is filled in ([prompt 16](../prompts/16-sources.md)).

| Id | What it does | In space | How it is checked on a mission |
|---|---|---|---|
| `brain` | Your brain runs your sleep, your mood, your attention and every movement you make. | Isolation, a shifted day and night, a heavy workload and radiation all press on sleep, mood and thinking speed. | Sleep logs, mood check-ins, and short tests of reaction time and thinking. |
| `eyes` | Your eyes turn light into the picture you see. | Body fluid moves toward the head in weightlessness. In many crew members on long missions the back of the eye changes shape, which can blur vision. | Regular vision tests and images of the back of the eye. |
| `ears` | The inner ear tells your brain which way is up and keeps you steady. The ear also hears. | With no up or down, the balance organs send confusing signals. That causes motion sickness in the first days and unsteadiness after landing. Constant machine noise can wear on hearing. | Balance and coordination tests, hearing tests, and noise measurements in the cabin. |
| `face` | Your nose and sinuses clean the air you breathe and give food most of its flavor. | Fluid moving toward the head makes the face puffy and the nose stuffy, like a mild cold. Food tastes dull and appetite can drop. | Asking how you feel, and watching how much you eat. |
| `heart` | Your heart pumps blood through vessels that reach every part of you. | Without gravity the heart has less work to do and blood shifts toward the head. Fitness drops unless you train, and standing up after landing can make you faint. | Heart rate, blood pressure, heart tracings and ultrasound scans. |
| `lungs` | Your lungs take in oxygen and remove carbon dioxide. | The lungs cope well, but a closed cabin can build up carbon dioxide, and dust floats instead of settling. The air you breathe matters more. | Blood oxygen from a finger or wrist sensor, breathing tests, and cabin air monitors. |
| `digestive` | Your stomach, liver and gut turn food into fuel and building material, with help from the bacteria that live there. | Crews often eat less than they need, and the mix of gut bacteria shifts in flight. | Food logs and body mass. |
| `kidneys` | Your kidneys filter your blood, balance water and salt, and make urine. | Bones release calcium in weightlessness and crews tend to drink too little. Together these raise the chance of kidney stones. | Tracking water and salt intake, and urine tests. |
| `spine` | Your spine holds you upright and protects the nerves that run to the rest of your body. | Without weight pressing down, the spine lengthens and the back muscles weaken. Back pain is common in flight, and the discs are more easily hurt after return. | Height and back-pain reports, and scans before and after flight. |
| `bones` | Bones carry your weight, protect your organs and store your calcium. | Unloaded bones lose mineral month after month, most of all in the hips, thigh bones and spine. Without sunlight the body makes no vitamin D. | Bone scans before and after flight, blood and urine markers during it, and calcium and vitamin D intake. |
| `muscles` | Muscles move you and hold your posture. | With no weight to carry, muscles shrink and weaken quickly, most of all in the legs and back. | Strength tests, exercise logs and body mass. |
| `reproductive` | The reproductive organs make sex hormones and the cells needed to have children. | Radiation is the main concern for these organs. Less is known here than for most systems, because few people have flown long missions. | Hormone levels in blood samples, and health checks before and after flight. |
| `blood` | Blood carries oxygen, nutrients and immune cells. | The body holds less blood fluid in space and breaks down red blood cells faster than on Earth. Iron stores tend to rise, so too much iron matters more than too little. | Blood samples. |
| `immune` | Your immune system finds and fights infections, through vessels and nodes spread across the whole body. | Stress, poor sleep and radiation change how immune cells behave. Viruses that normally lie dormant can wake up, and rashes and allergies are more common. | Blood and saliva samples, and a record of any illness or rash. |
| `skin` | Skin is your barrier against the outside and helps control your temperature. | Skin gets drier and more easily irritated, small wounds heal more slowly, and the soles of the feet soften because they are no longer walked on. | Skin checks and a record of rashes or wounds. Some watches read skin temperature. |
| `hormones` | Glands release hormones that set your energy use, stress response, body clock and blood sugar. | Stress hormones run higher, the body clock drifts without a normal day and night, and the body may handle sugar less well. | Blood, urine and saliva samples, plus sleep timing and body mass. |

A system never states a diagnosis. It shows the levels of the values it reads and what to do next: open the matching screen.

### The figure

Built in two passes, so there is always something that works:

1. **Working figure** ([prompt 14](../prompts/14-overview.md)). A plain outline with a marker for each organ system and chips for the whole-body systems. It makes the whole screen work end to end.
2. **Anatomical figure** ([prompt 15](../prompts/15-body-figure.md)). Replaces the plain one with a carefully proportioned human body with real organ shapes, a skeleton, muscle groups, the main blood vessels and lymph nodes, drawn as layers. This is a job for the strongest model available, because it needs visual judgment.

Both passes keep the same interface, so the screen does not change when the figure is swapped. The figure lives in its own folder, `app/src/screens/overview/figure/`, and exposes one component:

| `BodyFigure` input | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `systems` | The sixteen systems, each with `id`, `label`, `kind` (`organ` or `whole-body`) and `status` (`ok`, `watch`, `act`, `none`, `untracked`) |
| `selectedId` | The open system's id, or none |
| `onSelect(id)` | Called when the user chooses a system on the figure or in its chips |
| `heartRate` | Latest heart rate in beats per minute, or none. Used only to time the heart's beat animation |

What both passes must keep: every organ system can be chosen on the figure and every whole-body system from the chips; each control has an accessible name made of the label and the status label, for example "Kidneys and bladder, WATCH"; the chosen system is highlighted in its status tone; nothing moves when the system asks for reduced motion.

### Today's routine

Seven items, always in this order. Each is DONE, DUE or NOT PLANNED, worked out from data that already exists. Nothing is stored for the routine itself.

| # | Item | DONE when | Button when DUE |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Log last night's sleep | Today has a sleep value | "Open Sleep" |
| 2 | Heart-rate reading | The latest vital sample with a heart rate is from today | "Take a reading", opens Physical activity |
| 3 | Mood check-in | A check-in exists today | "Check in", opens Mood |
| 4 | Exercise | Every session planned today is done. Shows "1 of 2 sessions". NOT PLANNED if none is planned | "Open Physical activity" |
| 5 | Water | Water level is `ok` | "Add 250 ml", added on the spot |
| 6 | Vitamin D | Today has a vitamin D value above zero | "Open Nutrition" |
| 7 | Weekly weigh-in | A body mass value exists in the last 7 days | "Open Nutrition" |

On the demo profile the seeded day gives a mix: sleep, water partly, and one exercise session are in; the mood check-in is not. That mix is what makes the card worth showing.

When at least one item is DUE, Ophi shows his hint dot on this screen.

**Not on this screen:** cabin or environment readings (there is no data for them, and inventing it would break the rule against made-up values), editing of any record beyond the one-tap water button, and anything that names a medical condition.

**Guide text for Ophi:**

- **Shows:** your whole day on one page: how each part of you is doing, what is still to do today, and any open alert.
- **Why it matters:** this far from Earth, you are the first one who can notice a problem. A quick look every day catches small things early.
- **How to read it:** tap a part of the body to see what it does, what space does to it, and how it is doing. Green means on target, amber means watch it, red means act today. A dashed outline means this demo does not track that system. The routine card lists what is still due.
- **When to act:** work through anything marked DUE before the end of the day. If a body system stays red for several days, raise it with the flight surgeon. For anything urgent, use Emergency.

## Med bot

**Owner:** Rami. **Eyebrow:** `DOMAIN · MED BOT`. Built with [prompt 12](../prompts/12-medbot-chat-screen.md).

**Question it answers:** I have a health question right now. What does my data say, what can I do myself, and does this need a real medical professional?

**Why it matters in space:** on a mission beyond low Earth orbit a question to the ground can take many minutes each way, and the flight surgeon's time is limited. A crew member needs a first answer on board, and a clear signal for which questions must still go to a person.

**Med bot and Ophi Tov are two different things.**

| | Ophi Tov | Med bot |
|---|---|---|
| Where | The corner of every screen | Its own screen, and the Talk page on the watch |
| What it does | Explains the screen you are on, from text the screen's owner wrote | Answers the questions you type, using your summary if you allow it |
| How it works | Scripted. No language model | A language model behind the chat endpoint, with scripted replies when it is unreachable |
| Hand-over | His panel has a button, "Ask the Med bot about this" | Can forward a question to the flight surgeon |

**Layout, top to bottom:**

1. Header with pills: ONLINE or OFFLINE, and "N SENT TO SURGEON" when there are any.
2. A context chip, "Talking about: [screen]", when the person arrived from another screen.
3. The conversation, built from the shared chat components.
4. Suggested questions as chips.
5. The input, with the standing line "Demo assistant. Not medical advice."
6. "Sent to flight surgeon": the list of forwarded questions.

**Forwarding to the flight surgeon:**

- The Med bot decides, for each answer, whether the question should go to a medical professional: anything about a medicine or a dose, a symptom that has lasted days or is getting worse, a request for a diagnosis, a reading the person is worried about together with how they feel, or anything it is unsure of.
- When it decides yes, its reply says so in plain words and a card appears under it: "This is one for the flight surgeon", with a one-line summary the person can edit and a button, "Send to flight surgeon".
- Nothing is sent until the person presses the button. They can also forward any question themselves from a quiet action under each reply.
- A sent question shows in the list with its time, the summary, and "WAITING FOR REPLY". With a delay to Earth above zero, it also shows how long the signal takes each way. The person can withdraw it.
- A sent question is also an open alert, so it appears on Overview.
- It is a demonstration: the list says "Demonstration. Nothing is sent to a real person." The question stays on the device.
- An emergency is different. Anything urgent gets the Emergency card first, as before, and is not queued for later.

**Rules for tone and safety:** as in the Med bot's own prompt, [prompts/companion/medbot-system-prompt.md](../prompts/companion/medbot-system-prompt.md). No diagnosis, no doses, honest that it is a demo assistant.

**Links to other screens:** every screen's Ophi panel, Mood and Overview open it with the screen attached. It reads the health summary. Forwarded questions show on Overview.

**Out of scope:** voice on the console, images, conversation history after the tab is closed, a real flight-surgeon inbox.

### Guide text for Ophi

- **Shows:** a medical assistant you can ask about your health and your data, and the questions you have sent on to the flight surgeon.
- **Why it matters:** far from Earth, an answer from the ground can take a long time. A first answer on board helps you act, and tells you what must still go to a person.
- **How to read it:** type a question or tap a suggestion. A card under an answer means the Med bot thinks the flight surgeon should see it. The list at the bottom shows what you have sent.
- **When to act:** send anything about medicines, lasting symptoms or a worry that will not go away to the flight surgeon. For anything urgent, use Emergency, not the chat.

## Social

**Owner:** Rami. **Eyebrow:** `DOMAIN · SOCIAL`. Built with [prompt 17](../prompts/17-social.md).

**Question it answers:** how is my crew doing today, and who could use a word from me?

**Why it matters in space:** isolation and confinement is one of NASA's five hazards of human spaceflight. A small crew lives together for months, and how well they notice and support each other affects everyone's health and work. A quiet, honest signal of how each person is doing makes it easier to reach out early.

**What it is:** a small, private crew network. You find other people who use the app, send a friend request, and once they accept you can chat with them and see their mood. It is closer than a public feed: no photos, no likes, no posts, and nothing about anyone's health.

**What friends can see, and what nobody can:**

| | Who sees it |
|---|---|
| Your handle (a name you choose for the network) and your role | Anyone who searches |
| Your mood: the word for your latest check-in and up to two feelings, such as LOW · Irritable | Friends only, and only while "Share my mood" is on |
| Your chat messages | The friend you sent them to |
| Your real name, mood notes, readings, sleep, food, exercise, alerts, medical status | Nobody. They never leave your device through Social |

**Before joining:** one card. Choose a handle, read the table above in two sentences, set the "Share my mood with friends" switch (off until the person turns it on), press "Join the crew network".

**Layout after joining, at PC width, two columns side by side:**

- **Left, "Find crew":** a search field and the results under it. Each result shows the handle, the role and one action that depends on the relation: "Add friend", "Requested" (press to cancel), "Accept", or the word FRIEND. With nothing typed it shows suggestions.
- **Right, "Your crew":** a switch between two views, "Friends (n)" and "Requests (n)".
  - **Friends:** at the top, one line of crew mood, for example "Crew mood: 3 good or better, 1 low, 1 not shared". Then one row per friend: the handle, their mood as a face, a word and their feelings, how long ago, a source badge, an unread marker, and a "Chat" button. A friend whose mood is low or very low gets a quiet line, "A short message can help."
  - **Requests:** incoming requests with "Accept" and "Decline", then requests you sent with "Cancel".
- **Above both columns, "What your friends see":** your own shared mood exactly as friends see it, the sharing switch, and "Check in" which opens the Mood screen. When there is no check-in in the last 24 hours it says "No mood shared today".

**At phone width:** one column. "What your friends see" first, then a three-way switch: Find, Friends, Requests.

**Chat:** opens in the detail drawer (right side on PC, bottom sheet on phone). The friend's handle and mood at the top, the conversation, the input. Text only, 500 characters. "Remove friend" sits at the bottom behind a confirmation.

**The demo profile** has a simulated crew of six fictional people so the screen is alive for a judge who opens it alone: two friends with moods and a short conversation, one incoming request, three people to find. They answer chat messages with a few fixed replies. Everything about them is marked SIMULATED. A real profile never sees simulated people.

**Rules for tone and safety:**

- Mood is shown as a word and a face, never as a number and never by color alone.
- Never label a friend. "LOW" is the word they chose; the screen adds nothing to it.
- Sharing is the person's choice, off until they switch it on, and switching it off removes their mood from friends at once.
- The chat is not for emergencies. A fixed line under the input says "For anything urgent, use Emergency."
- "Leave the crew network" removes the account, the friendships and the messages.

**Links to other screens:** "Check in" opens Mood. Mood's "Message home" opens Social. The shared mood comes from the Mood check-in.

**Out of scope:** photos, posts, a feed, likes, group chats, voice or video, notifications when the app is closed, blocking and reporting beyond "Remove friend" and "Decline".

### Guide text for Ophi

- **Shows:** your crew: who you are connected to, how each of them is feeling today if they chose to share it, and your conversations.
- **Why it matters:** months in a small space far from home are easier when the crew looks out for each other. Seeing that a crewmate is having a hard day is the first step to helping.
- **How to read it:** search on the left to find people and add them. Your friends and requests are on the right. Each friend shows a mood word and a face. Tap Chat to write to them.
- **When to act:** if a friend's mood is low, send a short message. If you are worried about a crewmate's safety, tell the flight surgeon, and use Emergency for anything urgent.
